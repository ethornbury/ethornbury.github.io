Artist statement

My practice explores the relationship between the urban environment, sensory experience and the resilience of life within the city.
Working primarily in Dublin’s Docklands, I am drawn to the tensions within urban spaces: their capacity to stimulate and inspire, while also feeling hard, imposing and alienating.
I work across drawing, print, textiles and mixed media, using ink, graphite, charcoal, wire and found surface impressions. Street rubbings are central to my process, allowing me to engage physically with the textures of the built environment. Impressions of paving, stone, plaques and street signage become records of place, capturing details that might otherwise go unnoticed. Through mark-making, layering and repetition, I explore the boundaries between the built environment and the body, and the ways in which the city shapes our sensory and emotional responses.
My recent work shifts towards the quiet persistence of nature within this landscape. Grasses and mosses growing through cracks in pavements and along walls become powerful symbols of resilience, endurance and hope. These small interventions of life challenge the apparent permanence and dominance of concrete and stone. Combining printed textiles, sublimation printing, foil, surface manipulation and hand embroidery, I bring together contrasting materials and processes. Delicate threads and organic forms sit against harder, more industrial surfaces, creating a dialogue between vulnerability and strength, concealment and visibility, neglect and renewal. Through this work, I seek to draw attention to what is overlooked, finding moments of connection and possibility in the margins of the urban landscape.
